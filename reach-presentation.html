Executive summary

Reach is how Australian SMBs adopt reliable AI.

Two waves are colliding. AU SMBs feel pressure to adopt AI but have no reliable way to deploy it. At the same time, capable software engineers are being force-exited from big tech in record numbers and looking for a way to build an independent practice. Reach is the platform that turns those engineers into a trusted, branded delivery network for SMB AI — with product-grade infrastructure behind every deployment.

Why now

Off-the-shelf AI is already good enough for a large class of SMB workflows. The missing layer is a repeatable way to scope, configure, evaluate, brand, monitor, and support those systems in firms without internal AI teams.

Who delivers

Software engineers force-exiting big tech — technically credible, commercially under-equipped, looking for an independent practice. Reach gives them the surface to sell, deliver, and operate AI capability at SMB scale.

Who buys

Australian SMBs in three launch verticals: Document Intake (legal, accounting, migration), Customer Support (ecommerce, SaaS, services), and HR (20–500 staff, no dedicated HR leadership).

Two realities are colliding.

A high-density group of capable technical specialists wants independent practice. A large base of SMBs wants AI outcomes packaged as concrete, safe-to-buy capability.

Build approach

Use the OSS substrate. Accumulate IP above it.

STACKs should assemble from production-grade open-source architectures wherever the commodity layer is already good, while reserving proprietary effort for domain-specific deployment advantage.

Commodity substrate

  • Orchestration frameworks
  • Vector and graph stores
  • Evaluation harnesses
  • Observability
  • OCR and document AI
  • Self-hosted inference for cost-sensitive workloads

Proprietary layers

  • Vertical ontologies
  • Evaluation suites and failure libraries
  • Connector inventory and deployment patterns
  • Branded UX and workflow surfaces
  • Compliance posture
  • Deployment data flywheel

Frontier proprietary models are used selectively where accuracy-critical reasoning warrants the cost. The default bias is workable SMB unit economics.

Enough upside to build a practice, with Reach attached to product revenue.

Discussion

What we should pressure-test together.

1. Wedge confidence

Is Document Intake clearly the right first wedge, or is HR's domain edge worth pulling forward despite heavier activation?

2. Buyer clarity

Can an SMB buyer understand the outcome quickly enough, and does the consultant have a credible path to trust?

3. Consultant pull

Is the economic promise strong enough for capable technical people to build a practice on top of it?

4. Build risk

Which layer is most likely to be harder than expected: connectors, evals, compliance, UX, or operations?

5. Pricing

Are the activation fees, SMB MRR tiers, and commission rates coherent for both sides of the market?

6. Next proof

What is the smallest validation artifact: mock CORE flow, vertical diagnostic, paid pilot, or founder-led deployment?

Decision to make: is Reach a real venture thesis, a narrower productized agency tool, or a set of ideas that should be split before building?
